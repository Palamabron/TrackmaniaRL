## State-conditioned follow-up

`drive-neighbors` retains the long middle-corner intervention. At 63–90% it uses
the highest-Q action among 15 nearest reference states, using progress, speed,
forward/lateral velocity, yaw rate, heading and road-relative context. A distant
state (squared distance above 9) or speed below 15 m/s falls back to the source
policy. Leave-one-episode-out coverage excluded 15/402 recorded reference actions
(3.7%), with no distance fallbacks. This is coverage evidence, not a lap-time proof.

The live `drive-neighbors` screen failed. The 46.46 s lap had already lost speed
before the state-conditioned intervention began at 63% progress. The next
candidate, `neighbors`, removes forced action 75 entirely and applies the
state-conditioned action support throughout 54–90% progress. The network still
chooses the highest-Q supported action; references are fixed before benchmarking,
selected only from the checkpoint's existing replay, never from benchmark laps.

## Effect of dropped telemetry frames

All 458 skipped producer frames remain represented in the complete 30-trial
result. Producer skips are not equivalent to skipped policy decisions: the
controller can hold an action while consuming multiple producer frames. The
maximum observed interval between decision observations was 80 ms of race time.
Missing observations can change braking, steering and recovery timing, but these
measurements do not establish a fixed time penalty or isolate a hardware cause.

The 41.370 s attempt had a maximum measured clock step of 50 ms in each inspected
progress bin. This argues against a long telemetry stall as its explanation, but
does not prove that timing jitter had no influence on the trajectory. The baseline
had 578 skipped frames over only 10 attempts; different sample sizes, trajectories
and non-paired runs prevent a causal comparison using these totals alone.

Lap times use the reported race clock, not received-frame counts. Missing frames
are not subtracted from lap times and no time correction was applied. Clock
precision and terminal-event delivery still matter, as illustrated by the
36.560 s telemetry / 36.568 s game-overlay difference above. No zero-drop or
millisecond-exact game-clock claim is made. The 23.333 ms margin below 37 s is
small; neither sampling variability nor measurement uncertainty should be hidden.

For future comparisons, predeclare timing/skip limits and retain all attempts.
Reject a whole series if a strict telemetry gate fails, report why and rerun a
complete series after addressing the cause. Do not discard the laps with the
most skips or the slowest times. See the [evaluation contract](../library-architecture.md#dropped-telemetry-frames-and-evaluation-validity).

## Same-clock finish measurement bug

The confirmation captured a genuine finish flag at 36,810 ms immediately after
a nonterminal frame at the same 36,810 ms, with zero skipped frames. The old
validator counted this zero-duration terminal notification as an absent measurement.
The fix accepts zero only for a healthy `finished` notification. Environment step
diagnostics now retain signed differences, so clock regression cannot masquerade
as zero. Nonterminal zeros, missing values, booleans, NaNs and regressions remain
invalid. 35 targeted tests passed. Race time, rewards, physics and driving actions
are unchanged. This fix does not rescue the failed 29/30 confirmation.
